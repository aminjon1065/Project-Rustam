# 1. EXECUTIVE VISION

PROJECT RUSTAM is an ambitious original video game franchise inspired by the legendary world of Ferdowsi's Shahnameh and the historical, artistic, and cultural heritage of the Tajik-Persian civilization.

The project aims to transform one of the world's great epic traditions into a premium interactive entertainment experience for a global audience.

At its center is Rustam: a legendary warrior whose extraordinary strength, courage, loyalty, personal relationships, and tragic destiny provide the foundation for a deeply human story.

The franchise will explore themes of:

- Heroism and responsibility.
- Fatherhood and legacy.
- Honor and sacrifice.
- Fate and free will.
- Love, loyalty, and betrayal.
- The consequences of war.
- The relationship between extraordinary power and human weakness.

The project is not intended to reproduce the literary epic scene by scene.

Instead, it will adapt its essential narrative foundations into an original interactive experience, preserving the defining events, characters, and emotional truths while expanding the world, character development, gameplay opportunities, and cinematic storytelling.

**Our ambition is to create a globally recognizable game franchise that introduces the world to the extraordinary cultural and mythological heritage associated with Tajikistan.**

# 2. CORE CREATIVE CONCEPT

## 2.1 The Central Idea

A legendary hero is not defined only by the enemies he defeats, but by the choices he makes, the people he protects, and the consequences he cannot escape.

Rustam must be presented as more than an invincible warrior.

He is a human being living under the weight of an extraordinary destiny.

His physical strength may make him legendary, but his relationships, vulnerabilities, decisions, and mistakes must make him believable.

## 2.2 Creative Identity

PROJECT RUSTAM should establish an identity that combines:

- Epic mythological storytelling.
- Cinematic action and emotionally meaningful combat.
- Rich environmental exploration.
- Memorable characters and relationships.
- Distinctive Central Asian and Persian-inspired visual identity.
- A carefully constructed mythology.
- High-quality audiovisual presentation.

The project must not become a superficial imitation of existing Western or Asian fantasy franchises.

Its identity must originate from its own cultural and literary foundations.

# 3. FRANCHISE POSITIONING

## 3.1 Market Position

PROJECT RUSTAM is envisioned as a premium, narrative-driven, single-player action-adventure franchise with RPG elements.

Its creative ambition draws inspiration from the production qualities and storytelling approaches of:

- God of War — cinematic combat and emotionally driven character relationships.
- The Witcher — morally complex storytelling and mythological world-building.
- Ghost of Tsushima — environmental storytelling and visual coherence.
- Black Myth: Wukong — mythological identity and spectacular enemy encounters.
- Assassin's Creed — historical environments and cultural atmosphere.

These titles are references for studying design and production techniques, not templates to copy.

## 3.2 Target Audience

Primary audience:

- Players aged approximately 18–40.
- Fans of cinematic action-adventure games.
- Players interested in mythology and legendary heroes.
- Fans of narrative-driven single-player experiences.
- Players who appreciate sophisticated world-building and visual presentation.

Secondary audience:

- Historical and mythology enthusiasts.
- International audiences interested in Central Asian and Persian cultures.
- Players interested in emotionally complex stories.
- Communities connected to Tajik, Persian, and broader Central Asian heritage.

## 3.3 Platform Strategy

Initial target platforms:

- PC (Windows).
- PlayStation.
- Xbox.

Final platform commitments will depend on production capacity, commercial agreements, and technical feasibility.

No multiplayer requirement is established at this stage.

The initial creative direction prioritizes a strong single-player experience.

# 4. SOURCE MATERIAL AND ADAPTATION PHILOSOPHY

## 4.1 Primary Literary Foundation

The principal literary foundation is Ferdowsi's Shahnameh.

The Rustam cycle will be studied as a complete narrative tradition rather than treating the Rustam and Sohrab episode as the only possible foundation.

The research must cover:

- Rustam's origins and family.
- His relationship with Zal.
- His relationship with Rudaba and other important figures.
- The story of Rakhsh.
- His legendary trials and heroic adventures.
- His relationship with kings and political powers.
- His encounters with supernatural forces.
- His family and personal relationships.
- His confrontation with Sohrab.
- His later life and final destiny.

The precise scope of the first game will remain open until this research is complete.

## 4.2 Hybrid Adaptation Policy

The project adopts a hybrid adaptation model.

**Preserve:**

- The essential identities of major characters.
- Defining relationships.
- Major canonical events selected for adaptation.
- The emotional significance of the original tragedy.
- The distinctive mythological identity of the source material.

**Expand:**

- Character motivations and development.
- Events between major episodes.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Supporting characters.
- Gameplay-oriented encounters.
- World exploration.
- Dialogue and interpersonal relationships.
- The player's understanding of the world.

**Create original material where appropriate:**

- Additional quests.
- Original supporting characters.
- New enemy encounters.
- Expanded locations.
- Gameplay-driven conflicts.
- New narrative connections between established events.

Original additions must not casually contradict defining elements of the literary source.

## 4.3 Source Classification

All major narrative elements must be classified into three categories:

A. **Primary Epic Material:** Events and characters directly supported by the Shahnameh.

B. **Historical and Cultural Context:** Information supported by historical, archaeological, linguistic, or cultural research.

C. **Original Creative Interpretation:** New material created specifically for the game.

This distinction is essential for intellectual honesty and cultural authenticity.

# 5. NARRATIVE DIRECTION

## 5.1 Narrative Philosophy

The game must combine epic scale with intimate human storytelling.

Large battles and supernatural encounters should have personal and narrative significance.

The player should understand why a battle matters beyond defeating an enemy.

## 5.2 Narrative Priorities

1. Strong central protagonist.
2. Emotionally meaningful character relationships.
3. Carefully structured dramatic progression.
4. Memorable antagonists.
5. Strong narrative payoffs.
6. Coherent integration of gameplay and story.
7. Respect for the source material.

## 5.3 The Rustam–Sohrab Tragedy

The confrontation between Rustam and Sohrab is considered one of the franchise's central narrative foundations.

Its exact role in the first game is not yet established.

It may serve as:

- The climax of the first game.
- The conclusion of a multi-chapter character journey.
- A central event approached through multiple narrative perspectives.
- The foundation of a subsequent game centered on Sohrab.

The decision must be based on the complete narrative research and the selected structure of the franchise.

The tragedy must never be reduced to a simple boss fight or conventional victory sequence.

# 6. FRANCHISE ARCHITECTURE

PROJECT RUSTAM is intended to become a scalable narrative universe.

Potential future titles include:

**GAME I — RUSTAM**

The first major installment. Its narrative period and exact scope will be determined during pre-production.

**GAME II — SOHRAB**

A potential sequel exploring the story of Sohrab, potentially revisiting defining events from a different perspective.

**FUTURE INSTALLMENTS**

Potential expansion into other characters and legendary cycles of the Shahnameh.

Possible subjects include Zal, Simurgh, other legendary heroes, and mythological conflicts.

These are creative possibilities, not confirmed production commitments.

The franchise should maintain narrative continuity while allowing individual games to have their own emotional identity and complete dramatic structure.

# 7. WORLD AND CULTURAL IDENTITY

## 7.1 World-Building Philosophy

The world must feel like a coherent, ancient, lived-in civilization rather than a collection of fantasy-themed environments.

Its design should be informed by:

- Ancient Central Asian civilizations.
- Bactrian and Sogdian cultural history.
- Historical Persianate artistic traditions.
- Traditional architecture.
- Regional textiles and decorative arts.
- Historical weapons and craftsmanship.
- Landscapes of Central Asia.
- Mythological imagery and literary descriptions.

## 7.2 Geographic and Environmental Identity

Potential environmental directions include:

- High mountain ranges.
- Deep valleys and rivers.
- Ancient fortresses.
- Walled cities.
- Rural settlements.
- Caravan routes.
- Arid landscapes.
- Forested regions.
- Mythological and supernatural environments.

Specific locations and their historical foundations must be researched before implementation.

## 7.3 Cultural Authenticity

The game should distinguish between:

- Historically documented details.
- Literary descriptions.
- Regional cultural traditions.
- Artistic interpretations.
- Original fantasy elements.

Cultural identity must be integrated into architecture, character design, music, language, objects, and environmental storytelling.

It should never be limited to decorative patterns or exotic visual stereotypes.

# 8. ART DIRECTION

## 8.1 Overall Visual Ambition

The visual direction should target premium, cinematic realism with a distinctive mythological identity.

The goal is not to reproduce historical reality exactly, nor to create generic medieval fantasy.

The world should feel ancient, powerful, mysterious, and culturally recognizable.

## 8.2 Visual Pillars

**Pillar 1 — Monumental Scale**

Large landscapes, imposing architecture, ancient fortresses, and visually memorable environments.

**Pillar 2 — Human Realism**

Believable characters, detailed facial expressions, convincing anatomy, and physically coherent animation.

**Pillar 3 — Cultural Specificity**

Architecture, clothing, armor, materials, ornaments, and visual motifs grounded in researched traditions.

**Pillar 4 — Mythological Wonder**

Supernatural beings and legendary encounters must feel extraordinary and integrated into the world.

**Pillar 5 — Cinematic Composition**

Strong framing, lighting, environmental storytelling, and carefully directed action sequences.

## 8.3 Art Direction Deliverables

Future production documentation must include:

- Global visual reference board.
- Character concept art.
- Armor and weapon design.
- Environment concept art.
- Architecture references.
- Creature design.
- Material and texture direction.
- Lighting standards.
- Color scripts.
- Cinematic visual guidelines.

# 9. GAMEPLAY VISION

## 9.1 Core Gameplay Direction

The provisional gameplay direction is cinematic third-person action-adventure with meaningful RPG elements.

The experience should emphasize:

- Responsive melee combat.
- Powerful weapon interactions.
- Distinctive enemy archetypes.
- Challenging boss encounters.
- Exploration and discovery.
- Character progression.
- Equipment and customization.
- Environmental interaction.
- Narrative-driven missions.

## 9.2 Combat Philosophy

Rustam's combat must communicate exceptional physical power without reducing every encounter to button-mashing.

Combat should reward:

- Timing.
- Positioning.
- Defensive skill.
- Understanding enemy behavior.
- Weapon mastery.
- Strategic use of abilities.

The exact weapon selection, combat mechanics, and progression systems remain open.

## 9.3 Exploration

Exploration should reveal the world through:

- Environmental storytelling.
- Hidden locations.
- Ancient structures.
- Mythological encounters.
- Optional narrative content.
- Discoverable cultural details.

The final world structure — linear, semi-open, hub-based, or open-world — will be determined after narrative and production-scope analysis.

# 12. PRODUCTION PHILOSOPHY

PROJECT RUSTAM is an ambitious long-term project.

Its production should be organized around clearly defined milestones rather than arbitrary deadlines.

The project will not attempt full-scale production before its narrative, gameplay, visual direction, and technical foundations have been sufficiently validated.

## Development Stages

**Stage 1 — Foundation**

Creative direction, source research, narrative research, and initial franchise strategy.

**Stage 2 — Pre-Production**

Narrative Bible, Character Bible, World Bible, Game Design Document, Art Direction Bible, and Technical Design.

**Stage 3 — Prototyping**

Combat prototypes, character movement, camera, interaction, enemy AI, and environmental tests.

**Stage 4 — Vertical Slice**

A polished, representative gameplay sequence demonstrating the intended experience.

**Stage 5 — Production Planning**

Team structure, production budget, schedule, content pipeline, publishing strategy, and financing.

**Stage 6 — Full Production**

Game development, content creation, testing, optimization, and release preparation.

No production schedule or budget is approved by this document.

# 14. KEY RISKS

**Narrative Risk**

The literary source may be adapted too loosely or too literally.

Mitigation: Establish a source-based narrative research process and adaptation policy.

**Cultural Risk**

The visual interpretation may unintentionally mix unrelated traditions or create historical inaccuracies.

Mitigation: Consult qualified researchers and cultural specialists.

**Scope Risk**

An oversized open world or excessive content ambitions may exceed production capacity.

Mitigation: Validate the gameplay structure through prototypes and vertical-slice development.

**Production Risk**

A premium cinematic experience requires substantial art, animation, audio, engineering, and testing resources.

Mitigation: Establish realistic production stages and financing requirements.

**Originality Risk**

The game may be perceived as an imitation of existing franchises.

Mitigation: Develop a distinctive cultural, narrative, visual, and mechanical identity.

**Technical Risk**

High visual ambitions may exceed target hardware performance.

Mitigation: Benchmark early and establish performance budgets.

# 16. IMMEDIATE NEXT STEPS

The next phase is not full game production.

It is structured research and creative discovery.

**Research Track A — Shahnameh**

Study the complete Rustam cycle, identify major episodes, relationships, chronology, and alternative interpretations.

**Research Track B — Historical and Cultural Foundations**

Study relevant Central Asian history, archaeological evidence, traditional arts, architecture, weapons, textiles, and cultural practices.

**Research Track C — Game Design Research**

Analyze relevant action-adventure games, combat systems, narrative structures, exploration models, and production approaches.

**Research Track D — Visual Development**

Develop an initial visual reference library for the world, characters, armor, weapons, architecture, and mythological creatures.

**Research Track E — Narrative Selection**

Compare potential narrative periods and structures for the first installment using dramatic strength, protagonist development, gameplay potential, cultural significance, and production feasibility.

The result will be a research-backed recommendation for the first game's narrative scope.
